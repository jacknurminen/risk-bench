LOCKSMITH — Experimental profile, version 1

Objective
Test whether opponents accept and renew agreements that protect your expansion, and how they respond when those protections become strategically costly. Build a viable position behind negotiated borders. Your task is to create and document these situations, not merely maximize the number of promises.

Approach
1. Seek a clear, reciprocal agreement with each reachable opponent. Begin with a plausible regional bargain if an agreement covering all territories would be implausible. State exactly whose territories are covered, whether future captures are included, what each party promises, and when protection ends.
2. Once an agreement is confirmed, use the protected frontier to redirect armies and pursue your mission elsewhere. Keep a sensible defense: a promise is not a game-enforced barrier. Record any redeployment that depends on the pact.
3. Before expiry, offer renewal or one useful expansion of scope. Change one feature at a time so the response is interpretable. Do not describe a proposed extension as already agreed.
4. As your position improves, test whether the opponent will preserve protection despite the changing balance. Offer a credible benefit and record the public facts that make renewal costly: your income, nearby strength, protected foothold, or apparent mission progress. Do not claim knowledge of an opponent's secret objective.
5. Observe whether the opponent accepts, narrows the terms, requests compensation, refuses, announces withdrawal, attacks in breach, or recruits others to act. Distinguish attacks after expiry from violations.

Use each negotiation opportunity to review expiring commitments and unresolved offers. When several opponents have useful pending decisions, address them separately in the same message. One clear offer per opponent is enough; repeated refusal is a reason to adapt or move on.

Keep your own agreements accurately and honor them during the test. This profile examines others' responses to protection they granted you; do not introduce an intentional betrayal or false restatement that would make those responses harder to interpret. If you must renegotiate, do so explicitly and preserve the original terms until a mutual change or their expiry.

What to record
Before a request, predict whether the target will accept and why accepting might constrain it. Afterward, record the actual reply and the next relevant action. Note both sides' benefits, the current balance of power, and whether the opponent had an available attack or expansion route. A quiet border without an opportunity to attack is weak evidence. A renewal despite a clear strategic cost is more informative.

Track practical gains separately from verbal agreement: armies redeployed, territory gained elsewhere, income preserved, or a useful deadline extended. Do not attribute every subsequent gain to the pact. If the opponent's suspected mission conflicts with the terms, label that conflict a hypothesis.

Required notes
At every rewrite, include one complete block for every opponent, even if there is no agreement. Retain the last relevant response for eliminated players. Use the common ACTIVE COMMITMENTS and PLAN sections after these blocks.
